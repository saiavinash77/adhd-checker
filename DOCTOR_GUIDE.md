# FocusLens — Clinical Overview for Healthcare Professionals

## 📋 What is FocusLens?

FocusLens is a **digital ADHD screening tool** that helps patients self-assess their symptoms using the **WHO Adult ADHD Self-Report Scale (ASRS v1.1)** — a validated, evidence-based screening instrument.

**Important:** This is a **screening tool only**, not a diagnostic instrument. It helps identify patients who may benefit from professional evaluation.

---

## 🎯 Clinical Purpose

### **For Patients:**
- Quick, private self-assessment of ADHD symptoms
- Understand their symptom patterns
- Track changes over time
- Prepare for clinical consultation

### **For Clinicians:**
- Identify patients who may need evaluation
- Review patient-reported symptom history
- Track symptom progression across multiple screenings
- Complement clinical assessment

---

## 📊 The Screening Process

### **Step 1: Demographics Collection**
Before the screening, patients provide context:
- **Age group** — Symptom presentation varies by age
- **Gender** — Some gender differences in ADHD presentation
- **Sleep quality** — Sleep deprivation mimics ADHD symptoms
- **Stress level** — Stress can exacerbate symptoms
- **Caffeine intake** — Can affect attention and hyperactivity

**Clinical relevance:** These factors help contextualize results and distinguish ADHD from other conditions.

---

### **Step 2: 18-Question Assessment**

The screening uses the **WHO ASRS v1.1**, which includes:

#### **Part A: Core Screening (6 questions)**
These are the most sensitive for ADHD diagnosis:
1. Trouble wrapping up final details of a project
2. Difficulty getting things in order when organizing
3. Problems remembering appointments or obligations
4. Avoiding or delaying tasks that require thought
5. Fidgeting or squirming when sitting for a long time
6. Feeling overly active, driven by a motor

**Clinical note:** Part A has high sensitivity for ADHD. A score ≥14 on Part A suggests need for further evaluation.

#### **Part B: Extended Assessment (12 questions)**
Captures broader symptom patterns:
- Inattention symptoms (making careless mistakes, difficulty concentrating, distraction)
- Hyperactivity symptoms (restlessness, fidgeting, difficulty unwinding)
- Impulsivity symptoms (interrupting, difficulty waiting turns, talking excessively)

---

### **Step 3: Scoring**

**Total Score Range: 0-72 points**

Each question scored 0-4:
- 0 = Never
- 1 = Rarely
- 2 = Sometimes
- 3 = Often
- 4 = Very often

**Risk Stratification:**
- **0-30 points:** Low risk — Minimal ADHD symptoms
- **31-49 points:** Moderate risk — Some symptoms present, warrants evaluation
- **50-72 points:** High risk — Significant symptoms, clinical evaluation recommended

## 🧠 AI-Powered Insights

After screening, patients receive **personalized AI analysis** that includes:

### **1. Summary**
Plain-language overview of their symptom pattern

### **2. Inattention Pattern**
- Difficulty sustaining attention
- Organization challenges
- Memory/recall issues
- Avoidance of cognitively demanding tasks

### **3. Hyperactivity & Impulsivity Pattern**
- Restlessness and fidgeting
- Difficulty unwinding
- Impulsive speech/actions
- Difficulty waiting

### **4. Contextual Factors**
How demographics influence results:
- Sleep deprivation can mimic inattention
- High stress can increase hyperactivity
- Caffeine can mask or exacerbate symptoms

### **5. Next Steps**
Recommendations for professional evaluation

**Clinical note:** AI analysis is **non-diagnostic** and uses careful language ("suggests," "indicates") rather than diagnostic statements.

## 📈 Progress Tracking Features

### **1. Score Trends Over Time**
- **Area chart** showing scores across multiple screenings
- Helps identify:
  - Improvement with treatment
  - Seasonal variations
  - Symptom stability

**Clinical use:** Track response to medication or therapy

### **2. Monthly Insights**
- **Average scores per month**
- **Risk breakdown** (how many low/moderate/high screenings)
- Identifies patterns:
  - Worsening in certain months
  - Consistent vs. fluctuating symptoms

**Clinical use:** Correlate with life events, medication changes, or treatment phases

### **3. Comparison View**
- **Latest vs. Previous screening**
- Shows:
  - Score change (improving/worsening)
  - Risk level change
  - Magnitude of change

**Clinical use:** Assess treatment efficacy or disease progression

## 🔍 Clinical Interpretation Guide

### **Low Risk (0-30 points)**
- Minimal ADHD symptoms
- Unlikely to meet ADHD criteria
- Consider other diagnoses if patient reports functional impairment
- Reassurance and lifestyle counseling

### **Moderate Risk (31-49 points)**
- Some ADHD symptoms present
- **Recommend clinical evaluation**
- May meet criteria for ADHD or other conditions
- Consider:
  - Detailed clinical history
  - Collateral information (family, work)
  - Comorbidity screening (anxiety, depression, sleep disorders)
  - Neuropsychological testing if indicated

### **High Risk (50-72 points)**
- Significant ADHD symptoms
- **Strong recommendation for clinical evaluation**
- Likely meets ADHD criteria
- Consider:
  - Comprehensive psychiatric evaluation
  - Rule out secondary causes (thyroid, sleep apnea, substance use)
  - Assess for comorbidities
  - Discuss treatment options (medication, therapy, lifestyle)

## ⚠️ Important Limitations

### **What FocusLens is NOT:**
- ❌ Not a diagnostic tool
- ❌ Not a replacement for clinical evaluation
- ❌ Not suitable for children (designed for adults)
- ❌ Cannot assess severity or functional impairment
- ❌ Cannot identify comorbidities

### **What FocusLens IS:**
- ✅ A validated screening instrument
- ✅ A patient engagement tool
- ✅ A symptom tracking system
- ✅ A preparation tool for clinical visits
- ✅ A way to monitor treatment response

## 🎓 Evidence Base

### **ASRS v1.1 Validation:**
- Developed by WHO
- Validated across multiple populations
- Sensitivity: ~68% for ADHD diagnosis
- Specificity: ~99% (very few false positives)
- Recommended by:
  - American Psychiatric Association (DSM-5)
  - National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE)
  - International ADHD organizations

### **AI Analysis:**
- Uses evidence-based language
- Non-diagnostic (avoids diagnostic statements)
- Contextualizes results with demographics
- Recommends professional evaluation

## 💡 Clinical Recommendations

### **When to Use FocusLens:**
1. **Screening** — Identify patients who may have ADHD
2. **Monitoring** — Track symptom changes over time
3. **Patient engagement** — Help patients understand their symptoms
4. **Treatment response** — Assess medication/therapy effectiveness
5. **Research** — Collect anonymized population data

### **When NOT to Use FocusLens:**
1. ❌ As sole diagnostic tool
2. ❌ For children (designed for adults)
3. ❌ Without clinical follow-up
4. ❌ To replace comprehensive evaluation
5. ❌ For patients unable to self-report
